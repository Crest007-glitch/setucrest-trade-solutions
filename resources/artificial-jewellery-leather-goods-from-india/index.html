Artificial Jewellery, Leather Goods and Consumer Products from India

What buyers should check when sourcing artificial jewellery, leather goods, bags, gifts and lifestyle products from Delhi-NCR suppliers.

By Ashutosh Gupta·Updated 8 September 2026·Practical guide

Artificial jewellery, leather goods, bags, gifts and lifestyle products can be attractive for small-batch sourcing because they are visual, varied and often available through wholesalers. They also require careful checking: a product photograph can hide differences in material, plating, stitching, dimensions and packaging.

Artificial jewellery

CheckQuestions to ask
Base materialWhat metal or alloy is used? Are stones, beads and clasps consistent?
Finish and platingWhat finish is applied, and how should the product be stored to reduce tarnish?
Skin-contact suitabilityAre material or component declarations available for the destination market?
Design consistencyWill the supplier reproduce the same size, colour and stone placement?
PackagingAre pieces individually protected from scratches, moisture and tangling?
BrandingCan the supplier provide unbranded goods or apply the buyer’s lawful brand?

Artificial jewellery is well suited to Sadar Bazaar, Swadeshi Market and specialist Old Delhi lanes, but branded lookalikes and imitation logos should be avoided. Buyers should also avoid making unsupported claims such as “hypoallergenic,” “nickel-free” or “gold” unless the supplier can provide appropriate evidence.

Leather and faux-leather goods

CheckQuestions to ask
Material identityIs the item genuine leather, split leather, bonded leather, PU or another synthetic material?
ConstructionHow are seams, edges, zips, snaps, lining and handles finished?
DimensionsAre the internal compartments and usable dimensions consistent with the listing?
FinishDoes the colour transfer, surface crack or produce a strong odour after packing?
PackagingHow will the item be protected from moisture, pressure and surface scratches?
Private labelWhat is the MOQ and setup cost for embossing, labels, boxes and dust bags?

Paharganj and Nabi Karim are practical areas to investigate for bags and travel goods, while Karol Bagh and specialist manufacturers may be useful for different quality and customization levels. Treat “export quality” as a claim to be checked, not a specification by itself.

Bags, textiles and lifestyle products

  • Ask for fabric composition, GSM or thickness where relevant.
  • Check stitching density, seam strength, lining, handles and zippers.
  • Confirm colourfastness and washing or care instructions.
  • Measure folded and packed dimensions because freight is affected by volume.
  • Request carton photographs and a repeat-order reference sample.

Home décor and gifts

Home décor can offer strong design variety, but it may be fragile, bulky or difficult to standardise. Check dimensions, finish, hanging hardware, breakage protection, master-carton quantity and whether the product can be economically shipped to the destination.

Product and brand-risk checks

  • Do not source counterfeit branded goods or products using another company’s logo.
  • Do not use photographs that imply an official brand relationship without permission.
  • Ask whether the supplier owns the design or has the right to sell it.
  • Check destination labelling and material disclosure requirements before large orders.
  • Keep the approved product photograph, dimensions, material description and packing instructions with the quotation.

Best initial consumer-product shortlist

For a first sourcing test, consider a compact range rather than a catalogue of hundreds of items: fashion earrings and hair accessories, wallets and cardholders, passport covers, canvas or jute bags, travel pouches, notebooks, gift packaging and small non-fragile décor. These products are easier to compare and ship than heavy furniture, liquids, food, electronics or fragile glass.

Important: Product standards, labelling and import restrictions depend on the destination and exact product. Confirm the final requirements with a qualified customs or regulatory professional before commercial import.
