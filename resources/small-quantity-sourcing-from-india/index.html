Small-Quantity Sourcing from India Without Full-Container Orders

How overseas buyers and small businesses can source smaller quantities from India using wholesalers, mixed lots, samples and practical shipping routes.

By Ashutosh Gupta·Updated 8 September 2026·Practical guide

Small orders from India can work, but they need a different buying strategy from factory-scale procurement. The objective is not to force a factory to accept an uneconomical order. It is to identify a supplier route where quantity, price, packaging and freight remain practical.

Small quantity does not always mean low total cost. Freight, packing, payment charges and destination clearance can be a large part of the landed cost.

Four practical sourcing routes

RouteStrengthTrade-off
Wholesaler ready stockLow MOQ, quick availability and mixed designsLess customization and variable replenishment
Mixed carton or mixed lotLets a buyer test several designs or coloursUnit economics and assortment must be checked carefully
Small-batch manufacturerBetter consistency and customization potentialMay require a production MOQ and longer lead time
Consolidated export shipmentUseful when several products are sourced from different suppliersMore coordination, documentation and packing control are required

How to negotiate MOQ intelligently

  • Ask for the smallest quantity available from stock before asking for new production.
  • Combine colours or designs only when the supplier can pack and invoice them clearly.
  • Ask whether customization is possible on the outer packaging instead of the product itself.
  • Offer a realistic repeat-order plan, but do not promise volumes you cannot support.
  • Ask for the price at three levels: trial quantity, repeat quantity and larger quantity.
  • Separate the MOQ for product, colour, logo, packaging and carton.

Calculate the real landed cost

Use a simple internal calculation:

Landed cost = product cost + local transport + packaging + export handling + freight + insurance + destination duties/taxes + brokerage/local delivery

For a small order, freight may be charged by actual or volumetric weight. A lightweight but bulky product can therefore be more expensive to ship than a heavier compact product. Request carton dimensions and gross weight before accepting the quote.

Products that usually fit the small-order model

  • Artificial jewellery and fashion accessories
  • Wallets, belts, cardholders and passport covers
  • Small bags, pouches and travel organisers
  • Stationery, corporate gifts and paper products
  • Jute, canvas and lightweight textile products
  • Small home décor items that are not highly fragile

Heavy furniture, fragile glass, liquids, food products, batteries and regulated items usually need more careful planning and may not suit a first small order.

Use samples as a decision tool

A sample should answer specific questions: Is the material correct? Is the finish consistent? Does the product survive handling? Is the packaging suitable? Can the supplier reproduce it? A sample that looks good in a video is not enough for a repeat programme.

When to move from a wholesaler to a manufacturer

Move to a manufacturer when the buyer needs stable specifications, private labelling, repeated production, technical documentation or a lower unit cost at a higher volume. Continue using a wholesaler when the buyer values assortment, quick dispatch or low commitment more than perfect consistency.

Payment and responsibility

For SetuCrest-managed individual, small and medium orders, the product option and quotation are approved before payment is used to purchase or dispatch the goods. Product cost, actual shipping and the agreed handling margin should be shown separately so the buyer can understand the total.

Practical caution: A supplier’s willingness to sell a small quantity does not automatically establish quality, export capability or reliability. Keep the approved specification, quotation, payment record and packing evidence together.
